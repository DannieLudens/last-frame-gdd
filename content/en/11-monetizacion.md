# Monetization

Monetization depends on the platform and the audience. Last Frame is a short PC game, played in the browser on itch.io, for players aged 10 and up. Ads and in-game purchases are designed for phones and endless games; in a 5-to-10-minute PC game they would bring in almost nothing and would also clash with the game's message (Soul vs. Algorithm). That is why we propose a premium model in phases:

- **Phase 1, bootcamp (December 2026):** free on itch.io, with optional payment. By default itch.io keeps 10% of each payment, and the team can change that share (https://itch.io/updates/open-revenue-sharing). The goal is to get players, feedback and followers, not revenue.
- **Phase 2, 2027:** if the game is well received, a full downloadable version for Windows (more sets and animated memories) at US$4.99 on itch.io and Steam, keeping the browser version as a free demo. Steam charges US$100 to publish each game, which is recouped once the game passes US$1,000 in sales (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee), about 200 sales at US$4.99, and Steam keeps 30% of each sale.
- **Extra:** an optional US$2 supporter pack with a PDF artbook, wallpapers and the soundtrack.

### Projection (phase 2, first year, before taxes)

Price US$4.99: net per sale of US$4.49 on itch.io (90%) and US$3.49 on Steam (70%). Scenarios on Steam:

- **Pessimistic:** 300 copies = about US$1,048.
- **Base:** 1,500 copies = about US$5,240.
- **Optimistic:** 5,000 copies = about US$17,465.

Covering the estimated production cost (about US$5,790) takes around 1,660 copies on Steam or 1,290 on itch.io. Little money is expected in phase 1: if 1,000 people play it and 2% pay US$2 on average, that would be about US$36 net.
