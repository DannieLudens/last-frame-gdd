# Overview

## Elevator pitch

You are a 1980s cartoon, forgotten after your show was cancelled and trapped in the closed room of a studio now ruled by AI-generated cartoons. Hide in the shadows, fight back with your ink pen and your correction fluid, and recover in every set the tape that holds one of your memories before The Replacement, your perfect copy, erases you forever and you fall into oblivion.

## Design pillars

```pilares
Soul vs. Algorithm | The core of the game is contrast. Ollie and the world are hand-made (organic lines, pencil strokes, uneven colors, some sketches finished and others half done). The enemies are AI animations: perfect, cold, generic, shiny and soulless. The player should feel the dignity of original art against mass production.
Survival through Stealth (Gameplay) | You are not an action hero; you are an unfinished toon. Direct combat is punished. The player must use darkness, crouch to stay quiet and hide behind the sets to avoid being detected by cameras and AI patrols.
Attack system | You have two attack options: a ranged attack with the ink pen, to get rid of distant enemies and break their formation, and a melee attack with the correction fluid, to erase your enemies.
```

## Summary

### Brief description

Last Frame is a 3D stealth game with a high-angle camera (from above and at an angle, not fully top-down) and the look of a classic 1980s cartoon. The player controls Ollie, a forgotten cartoon: the star of a cancelled show who wakes up in the classics room of an animation studio, closed for years and now guarded by AI-generated cartoons that are smiling, perfect and printed on paper.

A run goes through 5 sets of the studio. The first one teaches the mechanics, the three in the middle come in random order and the last one is the showdown with The Replacement. In every set Ollie looks for a tape with one of her memories and for the exit door, without being erased. She hides in the dark, avoids noise and, when there is no other way, attacks with her ink pen or her correction fluid.

### Detailed description

Seen from Ollie's size, the studio is huge: prop boxes, old cameras, animation desks and sheets of paper are walls and hiding spots. Each set is a different recording room. Set 1 works as a tutorial; when she crosses its door, the game randomly picks the order of sets 2, 3 and 4, so there are 6 possible routes. Set 5 is the set of potential ideas, where The Replacement waits.

The AI cartoons patrol, listen and watch. If they see Ollie, they chase her to erase her with their eraser. Each hit takes one of her 3 hearts and makes her bleed ink: for about 6 seconds she leaves a trail of drops that enemies can follow, and hiding does not remove it. If she loses all 3 hearts, she is erased: game over and back to the main menu. Each set also has a time limit before the door is sealed. The studio cameras sweep the room and call an enemy with an alarm. Spotlights expose her and darkness protects her.

Ollie can walk silently, run (fast but noisy) or crouch (slow and almost invisible). Her main weapon is a fountain pen as big as she is, which throws ink drops in an arc toward the cursor; with Q she swaps it for a correction fluid pen for melee attacks. Ink is scarce and carries over from one set to the next. Enemies are made of paper: ink stains them and dissolves them.

Contrast is the visual heart of the game: Ollie and the studio have pencil lines and warm, uneven colors; the enemies are shiny, symmetrical and generic. The intended feeling is quiet tension with the humor of a classic cartoon.

**First two minutes:** after choosing Play, an old tape shows the last frame in which Ollie was on the air. She wakes up in set 1, in the dark. Hand-painted signs teach her to move, crouch and hide; an AI cartoon walks by smiling with its eraser and the player learns to wait in the shadows. She finds her pen, tries an ink shot against a can to create a distraction, swaps to the correction fluid with Q, picks up her first tape (a short memory) and goes through the door to the next set.

## Theme, setting and genre

- **Genre:** stealth with light action, 3D with a high-angle camera, single player.
- **Theme:** handmade art against automated production; the fear of being forgotten and replaced.
- **Setting:** the classics room of a 1980s TV and animation studio, closed for years, seen at the scale of a small cartoon. 5 closed, maze-like sets, not an open world.
- **Tone:** classic cartoon, light tension and visual humor, for all ages.

## Main objective

Escape the studio without being erased: go through the 5 sets, recover the tape with one of her memories in each one and defeat The Replacement to go back on the air in a renewed show.

## Aesthetics, dynamics and mechanics (MDA)

**Aesthetics (what the player should feel):** tension while hiding, relief when getting past unseen, curiosity about the memories and sympathy for a cartoon fighting not to disappear.

**Dynamics:** watching patrols from the shadows, distracting with noise or an ink shot, choosing between sneaking past or attacking, managing the little ink and the 3 hearts, and shaking off enemies when they follow her ink trail.

**Mechanics:** movement with three speeds; detection by sight, hearing and trail; light and dark zones; cameras with alarms; ink pen with limited ammo; melee correction fluid; weapon swap with Q; 3 hearts; 6-second ink trail; one tape per set; random order of sets 2 to 4; final boss.

## Influences

Works that inspire the tone, the style and the mechanics. The Miro board also includes Metal Gear Solid (stealth and alerts), Epic Mickey (ink, paint and forgotten cartoons), Signalis (angled camera and scarce resources), Paper Mario (paper characters in a 3D world) and Octodad (physical humor and hiding in plain sight).

### Cuphead

- A handmade classic cartoon as the main hook of an independent game.

### Mark of the Ninja

- Stealth that is easy to read: clear light and shadow, visible noise on screen and the option to get through without fighting anyone.

### Bendy and the Ink Machine

- An abandoned animation studio and ink as the material of the world. Last Frame takes the setting, but with stealth for all ages instead of horror.

### Toy Story

- A character's fear of being replaced by a newer, shinier one, in an everyday world seen at a giant scale.

## What sets this game apart?

- The debate between handmade art and AI-generated content becomes a game rule: Ollie hides from perfect, generic enemies.
- Stealth for all ages: there is no blood; ink stains and dissolves the paper enemies, and Ollie bleeds ink when she is hit.
- Every run changes: sets 2 to 4 come in random order, and each tape tells a memory that stands on its own.

## Target audience

Players aged 10 and up who enjoy short, cartoon-style stealth games, and young adults interested in classic animation and in the conversation about AI and creativity. Short runs of 5 to 10 minutes, on PC with keyboard and mouse, in the browser.

## Expected rating

ESRB E10+ (Everyone 10+): mild cartoon violence, no blood and no offensive language. Proposal: before the intro, a rating screen like the ones in PlayStation 2 games, followed by the team logo.
